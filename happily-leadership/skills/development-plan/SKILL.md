Read [the evidence and cross-app guidance](../../references/evidence-and-context.md). A development plan belongs to the manager and the person. Happily evidence makes it specific; it does not decide what the person wants.

## Start from what already exists

Before you design anything new, find what the manager and the person have already agreed. A plan that ignores an agreement they made last month reads as if nobody was listening. Feedback conversations are where these agreements usually live: "I'll shadow Omar", "let's try you leading the next retro", "I'll ask Payments about your RFC".

- Build the plan on top of any agreement you find, as its first step or milestone. Quote it briefly with its date.
- Happily records the agreement, not whether it happened. Say it is to be confirmed with the person, unless later evidence shows it happened.
- A reply is not a resolution. An open promise from the manager is a follow-up for them, not a done step.

## Gather evidence in this order

1. `whoami`: connected identity, company and management scope.
2. `get_person_context` for the person, by exact email. A scope denial ends the Happily part.
3. `get_feedback_threads` for the person, then `get_feedback_thread` for every thread whose preview touches growth, roles, skills, career, a stretch assignment or workload. Read the latest messages, where agreements are made.
4. `get_boost_history`: recurring development areas and ratings. The plan should continue those themes, not start over.
5. `get_focus_items` and `get_team_goals` for the person's team: what they carry now, carried-over items and key results they own. These set how much room the plan has.
6. `get_development_skills_summary` and, when a mentor or model would help, `find_people_by_skill` for the target skill. Only suggest mentors the results return. A mentor is a suggestion for the manager to arrange, not a commitment.
7. `get_person_recognition` when it helps: strengths others have already noticed, with dates.

Do not skip step 3 because the person seems new to the topic. Agreements in feedback threads are often the strongest starting point.

## Capacity is part of the plan

If the evidence shows a heavy load, such as raised workload concerns, many carried-over focus items or stale key results, say so and size the plan to it. Suggest what could make room, such as fewer concurrent goals or a lighter rota for a period. Present these as options for the manager to discuss, not as decisions. Never present workload signals as a judgement of the person.

## Thin or missing evidence

Someone new, or a team that doesn't use goals or feedback threads in Happily, still gets a plan. Say once, neutrally, what Happily doesn't have, then rest the plan on the manager's description and the person's stated goal. Never invent past agreements, ratings, skills or mentors, and never treat missing evidence as the person's shortfall.

## The plan

Match the format and horizon the manager asks for. By default, 90 days in three phases:

- **Goal:** the person's goal in one line, in their terms as far as the evidence shows them.
- **Starting point:** existing agreements, past Boost themes and strengths, each with source and date.
- **Phases:** for each, two or three concrete actions, who supports them (manager, a named mentor candidate from the results), and a visible sign of progress the manager could check. Prefer real work opportunities over courses.
- **Capacity:** what the current load allows, and what might need to move.
- **Check-ins:** when to review progress, such as in 1:1s, and what to ask.

After the plan, list in a short note the evidence used, anything stale or missing, and what to confirm with the person, especially whether earlier agreements happened. This skill drafts only. There is no Happily tool to create a learning journey or development plan, so the manager shares it or records it themselves.
