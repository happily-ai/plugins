Read [the evidence and cross-app guidance](../../references/evidence-and-context.md), then establish identity, company and scope with Happily `whoami`. Use the user's priorities and requested period; otherwise state a seven-day lookback and seven-day lookahead in their timezone. If a timezone cannot be established, ask before interpreting calendar dates. Distinguish priorities stated by the user from your recommendations: use "Suggested priorities" when you inferred them. A first brief establishes a baseline; do not call an existing issue newly emerged without earlier dated evidence.

Start with `get_leader_digest` for the period: what moved on key results (and what reached target), what slipped or went quiet, finished work whose number wasn't updated, people with a win who haven't been recognized lately, places a goal or key result would help, and feedback waiting for a reply. Use `get_goals_overview` for the wider execution picture and `get_team_health` for a known authorized team. Leadership/admin can use `get_org_hotspots` to prioritize team-level follow-up; managers should stay within their authorized teams. Use `get_goal_alignment_overview` or `get_kr_progress_history` when a particular execution question warrants it. Follow up on relevant health signals with `get_pulse_trend`, `get_participation_status`, question trends, or feedback aggregates; use their actual schemas and supported periods. Recognition and development tools can identify positive developments as well as concerns. Do not query all tools or rank all people by default.

## Retrieve context that can change a decision

For a weekly brief with authorized email access, read this manager's current Weekly Insights for Managers email and its reporting period. Follow [the recurring-work guidance](../../references/proactive-leadership.md) for authenticating the report, handling late arrival and avoiding repetition. Use it as a dated starting point: add subsequent evidence, verify relevant commitments, and connect recommendations to upcoming decisions. A report's proposed action is not proof of an accepted commitment. If the current report is missing, disclose that gap; never silently substitute last week's report.

If Calendar is available, find relevant upcoming 1:1s, leadership reviews and deadlines across relevant authorized work accounts; deduplicate shared events and skip cancellations. Select the two or three most useful opportunities to act, rather than recap the entire calendar. A title alone is not enough to establish a meeting's business purpose, owner, or relation to a Happily goal. Confirm that the company, team or project matches before connecting evidence across accounts.

Inspect those events' descriptions and attachments. When authorized tools are available, actually read relevant linked agendas, meeting notes and reports; a discovered link or search snippet is not a read source. Follow the most relevant prior conversation through meeting notes, Drive or work communications. Start with up to five relevant records and expand only to resolve a specific material uncertainty. A rolling document can be useful, but cite the dated entry used and do not treat an old entry as this week's commitment. Do not conclude that only one meeting note exists merely because one source or search returned one result.

Look for what was agreed, who owns it, when it was due, and any later evidence of completion or change. Check an appropriate authorized source for completion when it changes the recommendation; otherwise say "completion not verified," not "overdue" or "unfinished." Use linked reports or scoped CRM/project/finance sources for business measures only when needed; Happily does not supply revenue, pipeline or cash flow. If a useful document cannot be accessed, disclose that specific gap and continue with available evidence.

Resolve responsible people from explicit goal ownership, directory/team relationships or dated commitments, and match exact email or verified identity before joining person data. Attendance, name similarity or a meeting title does not prove ownership. If ownership is unresolved, propose confirming an owner at a named meeting instead of inventing one or saying only "ask the team lead."

For a person-specific concern, check `get_feedback_threads` and read the relevant `get_feedback_thread` replies before recommending follow-up. These tools expose only feedback delivered to the connected manager's own inbox: named threads from their management subtree, and anonymous threads with no identity, which must never be attributed. Report anonymous feedback in its own section, never under, beside or as context for a named person's item, even with a disclaimer, and never suggest raising its theme with a particular person. They do not expose every employee conversation. Select threads that could change a decision; do not scan everyone's messages. Distinguish a new concern, a manager response, an accepted commitment, and explicit evidence of resolution. Read later replies before quoting the initial concern. Missing or inaccessible threads are a coverage gap, never proof that nobody responded. Cite the thread ID and relevant message dates; never invent a conversation link.

## Connect prior commitments, current evidence and the next opportunity

For each leading recommendation, seek a supported chain: **prior commitment or decision → current Happily/business evidence → upcoming opportunity to act**. State which links are established. Where a link is missing, label the connection as tentative and give the specific question that would resolve it. Do not force a three-source story when the evidence supports only a standalone observation.

Choose recommendations because the connection changes a decision: a previous acquisition commitment with no current progress evidence before the owner's next review; a documented support request plus a new team signal before a 1:1; a completed commitment worth recognizing. Avoid generic agendas such as "confirm blockers" unless evidence identifies the blocker or the information needed. Keep causal explanations tentative. A crowded calendar does not establish burnout; low engagement does not identify its cause; stale KR reporting does not establish poor business performance. Check whether old-period or completed KRs still belong in the active plan before recommending updates to all of them.

Resolve conflicting dates/statuses or state the disagreement. For each quoted metric, identify the team/entity, scale or unit, comparison periods, available sample size and relevant freshness limits. Do not imply statistical or practical significance merely because a score changed. For example, 47 to 51 on a 0–100 scale is a four-point increase, not a four-percent increase. Treat a modest positive movement as an encouraging signal to investigate unless other evidence establishes a meaningful win. Different metric series must remain distinct; do not silently reconcile them.

## Deliver a concise, traceable brief

Lead with at most three suggested priorities, ordered by impact, time sensitivity and evidence strength. For each, give the specific observation, why it matters now, and one next step with a verified owner (or explicit ownership gap), timing and intended outcome. Include a meaningful win when supported, without manufacturing one to fill a template. Do not add a generic meeting table that repeats these recommendations. Include a calendar conflict when resolving it changes the user's ability to act.

Put a compact source reference beside every material factual claim and proposed cross-source connection. Link actual meetings and documents returned by tools; for a document cite the entry date, not just the file name. When Happily returns no human-facing URL, use a plain-language label with tool name, entity and period/as-of date, e.g. "Happily get_goals_overview · authorized subtree · checked 26 Oct · 30-day update threshold." Never invent links. A coverage paragraph at the end does not replace claim-level sources.

Keep observed facts, your interpretation and proposed actions distinguishable. End with only material coverage limits and unverified links; do not call unread or unsearched sources unavailable. If a prior dated brief exists, identify new, changed and resolved findings within comparable coverage. If no issue merits attention, say so within the evidence limits.

For an authorized recurring responsibility, follow [proactive leadership](../proactive-leadership/SKILL.md), its saved notification rules and the shared dated baseline. Do not create a schedule just because the request says “weekly brief”; generating this week's brief and scheduling future runs are separate actions. Prepare follow-up drafts when helpful; do not send them without authorization.
